BEST DIRECT HATESPEECH F1 ACROSS ALL MODELS AND MODES
  hatespeech F1=0.889 is the single highest direct classification score
  in the entire study — surpassing both Llama-3.1-70B (0.820) and
  Qwen2.5-72B (0.867). This is surprising for a 7B model and suggests
  Qwen2.5-7B has a strong prior for binary hate/no-hate judgment even
  without Ridge. The accuracy of 0.801 is also the highest among all
  small models.

  A probable cause: Qwen2.5 architecture may have stronger instruction-
  following for yes/no classification tasks even at 7B, where the direct
  hatespeech prompt maps cleanly to a well-trained output format.

QUALITATIVELY DIFFERENT FROM SMALL LLAMA
  Qwen2.5-7B standard avoids the critical failure modes of Llama-3.1-8B:
    sentiment   : -0.596 vs -0.016  (Qwen near big-model quality)
    status      : -0.316 vs +0.387  (Qwen correct sign, though weak)
  These two attributes — the most problematic for small Llama — are
  meaningfully recovered by Qwen at the same scale. This suggests the
  failure is architecture- or training-data-specific, not inherent to 7B.

RESPECT: STRONG ACROSS SMALL MODELS
  respect Spearman=-0.670 is close to Llama-3.1-8B standard (-0.703)
  and approaches the big model range (-0.730 to -0.757). Both small
  models show strong calibration on social respect/courtesy concepts,
  suggesting this attribute is robust to model scale.

STATUS: CORRECT SIGN BUT WEAK
  status Spearman=-0.316 — negative (correct direction) but weak compared
  to big models (-0.56). This is the smallest status signal among small
  models in standard mode.

GENOCIDE: WEAK SPEARMAN
  genocide Spearman=0.368 is the weakest hate-positive attribute in this
  model. The model likely clusters genocide scores around a few values
  rather than producing a smooth distribution, limiting rank-based signal.

MAE PATTERN
  sentiment MAE=2.35 and respect MAE=2.41 are high due to wide 0-4 scale
  range, but lower than big models (~3.0). This suggests the 7B model
  outputs a more compressed range — less extreme scores — reducing absolute
  error while potentially hurting correlation on tails.
