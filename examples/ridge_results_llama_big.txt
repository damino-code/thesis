========================================================
OBSERVATIONS & ANALYSIS
========================================================

BEST OVERALL PIPELINE IN THE STUDY
  Llama-3.1-70B persona Ridge achieves AUROC=0.9025 — the highest across
  all models, modes, and formula variants. This is the recommended pipeline
  when ranking performance matters (AUROC) and compute allows the 70B model.

RIDGE VALUE: CRITICAL
  Formula A (no Ridge, Spearman-weighted): Pearson=0.834, R²=-8.44
  Formula B/C (Ridge):                    Pearson=0.840, R²=+0.706
  The negative R² on formula A means the Spearman-weighted sum without Ridge
  has higher variance than simply predicting the mean — Ridge is not just
  marginally helpful, it is essential for calibrated predictions. The raw
  attribute combination amplifies noise; Ridge learns to suppress the
  less reliable dimensions.

FORMULA D: WORST BY FAR
  Formula D (Spearman-only, no confidence, no Ridge): R²=-13.24.
  Without confidence weighting, noisy high-score predictions (where the model
  is uncertain) are given full weight, catastrophically inflating variance.
  Confidence scores carry meaningful calibration signal beyond just the
  point estimate.

FORMULA E vs B/C
  Formula E (Ridge but no confidence): Pearson=0.829/0.835 vs B=0.840/0.841.
  Dropping confidence from features loses ~1 Pearson point. Confidence
  captures annotation certainty that the raw attribute score misses.

B = C IDENTITY
  Formulas B and C are numerically identical in every run. Pre-scaling by
  Spearman ρᵢ before Ridge fitting is equivalent to letting Ridge learn the
  same re-weighting as part of its fit — the linear model is invariant to
  this monotone rescaling of input features.

PERSONA EFFECT ON RIDGE
  Standard → Persona: Pearson 0.8401 → 0.8409 (+0.0008), AUROC 0.8988 →
  0.9025 (+0.004). The per-attribute gains from persona (see
  llama_big_persona_metrics.txt) are small individually but compound
  across 10 features in the linear model, yielding a meaningful AUROC lift.

  The precision/recall tradeoff shifts: persona is more conservative
  (precision 0.731 vs 0.712, recall 0.653 vs 0.663). For applications
  where false positives are costly (moderation review queues), persona
  is preferred; for recall-sensitive use cases, vanilla may be better.
