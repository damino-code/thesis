OBSERVATIONS & ANALYSIS
========================================================

PERSONA EFFECT
  Persona mode injects annotator demographics (age, gender, race,
  ideology) into the prompt. For Llama-3.1-70B this produces
  consistent but modest improvements across most attributes:
    sentiment  : -0.70 → -0.72 (+0.02)
    respect    : -0.73 → -0.76 (+0.03)
    insult     :  0.70 →  0.72 (+0.02)
    humiliate  :  0.66 →  0.67 (+0.01)
    attack_defend: 0.64 → 0.69 (+0.04)  ← largest gain
  The gains are real but small, suggesting the 70B model is already
  capturing most of the signal from text alone.

NOTABLE IMPROVEMENTS
  attack_defend gains the most (+0.04 Spearman).
  This attribute — whether the comment defends or attacks a group —
  may be more context-dependent and benefit most from knowing the
  annotator's own in-group vs out-group perspective.

  MAE also improves across most attributes: sentiment 3.01→2.87,
  respect 2.91→2.83, status 2.02→1.86. The persona context appears
  to reduce systematic over-scoring.

REGRESSIONS
  violence MAE increases slightly (0.77→0.88) and Pearson drops
  (0.69→0.66). The persona prompt may introduce noise for this
  attribute — annotator identity matters less for judging explicit
  violent language than for subjective social-status attributes.

  hatespeech F1 drops very slightly (0.820→0.815), within noise.

RIDGE CONSEQUENCE
  Despite modest per-attribute gains, persona mode yields the best
  Ridge AUROC (0.9025 vs 0.8988 vanilla) because even small
  improvements across 10 features compound in the linear model.
  This is the best-performing model overall on the Kennedy dataset.
