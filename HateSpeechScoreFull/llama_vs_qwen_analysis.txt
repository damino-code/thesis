================================================================
  HATE SPEECH SCORE — Llama-3.3-70B vs. Qwen2.5-72B
  Why Llama wins the multivariate fit
================================================================

DATA
  Setup        : 39,565 unique comments (aggregated across annotators)
  Target       : mean IRT hate_speech_score per comment
                 (range [-8.34, 6.30], mean = -0.944, std = 2.073)
  Features     : score_adj_i = attribute_i × confidence_i, per attribute
  Model        : Ridge regression (alpha grid: 1e-4 ... 100)
  Validation   : pure 5-fold CV (4 folds fit / 1 fold test, rotated)

OVERALL RESULTS  (5-fold Ridge R^2, standard mode)
  Llama-3.3-70B : 0.7055 +/- 0.006   (best alpha = 100)
  Qwen2.5-72B   : 0.6891 +/- 0.006   (best alpha = 100)
  Gap           : 0.0164  (consistent across folds; ~3 fold-stds apart)

  Ridge needs spread in the predictors to fit a slope, so a compressed
  predictor caps how much variance it can explain.

----------------------------------------------------------------
  3. WHY THE MULTIVARIATE GAP  (where the 0.0164 R^2 comes from)
----------------------------------------------------------------
  The IRT hate_speech_score blends two axes:

    Axis A — Politeness <-> Rudeness    (sentiment, respect, insult)
    Axis B — Hate severity              (dehumanize, humiliate,
                                         attack_defend, violence,
                                         hatespeech)

  Qwen's three strongest signals all live on Axis A. Adding the 2nd and
  3rd axis-A attributes buys Ridge very little extra variance because
  they are highly correlated with each other -- pure redundancy.

  Llama's top three already span both axes (rudeness + direct attack),
  and then `dehumanize` (solo R^2 = 0.554) comes in 4th carrying genuine
  Axis-B information that Qwen cannot match (Qwen's `dehumanize`
  R^2 = 0.431 -- a 0.124 gap).

  Net effect:
    - Qwen wins +0.04 R^2 on Axis A (respect, sentiment).
    - Llama wins much more on Axis B (dehumanize +0.124, attack_defend
      +0.049, hatespeech +0.053, violence +0.038, humiliate +0.040).
    - The target depends on both axes; Ridge picks up Llama's
      complementary Axis-B signal while Qwen runs out of new variance.

----------------------------------------------------------------
  4. WHAT THIS IS NOT
----------------------------------------------------------------
  - Not a confidence issue.
      Qwen's mean confidences are roughly equal or slightly higher
      (e.g., respect 0.90 vs 0.87, insult 0.87 vs 0.81). Multiplying
      by confidence does not penalise Qwen.

  - Not a per-attribute correlation issue at the row level.
      Several pairwise corr(attr, target) values favour Qwen
      (insult, sentiment, respect). The advantage only appears once
      Ridge fits multiple attributes jointly and has to find
      *complementary* signal.

  - Not a "Llama is a better hate detector" claim.
      Qwen is slightly better at scoring the politeness/rudeness axis
      in isolation. On a binary classification target (hate vs. not),
      the gap could shrink or invert.

----------------------------------------------------------------
  5. ONE-LINE SUMMARY
----------------------------------------------------------------
  Llama wins because it provides Ridge with diverse, well-spread
  predictors across both axes of the IRT score; Qwen concentrates its
  predictive power in one redundant axis and gives up ground on
  hate-severity attributes (especially dehumanize), which is precisely
  where the IRT target needs help.
